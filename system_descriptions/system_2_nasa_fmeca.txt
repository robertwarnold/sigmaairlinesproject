System: NASA Concept Tilt-Wing Propulsion System

Purpose:
Supply and control power to four rotors for vertical and forward flight.

Components:
1. Turboshaft engine - drives the electrical generator.
2. AC generator and AC/DC converter - supply propulsion electricity and low-voltage flight-control power.
3. Battery network - stores electrical energy for propulsion.
4. Flight control computers - command individual electronic speed controllers.
5. Electronic speed controllers - regulate electric motors and use internal DC/DC converters for their own power.
6. Four electric motors - drive the rotor powertrains.
7. Sprag clutches - provide overrunning connections in the motor drive paths.
8. Accessory gearboxes - drive cooling fans and lubrication pumps.
9. Rotor gearboxes - reduce motor speed and transmit torque to rotors.
10. Interconnecting shafts - provide a secondary power path and rotor synchronization.
11. Cooling fans, heat exchangers, and lubrication pumps - cool and lubricate motors and gearboxes.

Operating conditions:
Hover and cruise; batteries are charged before flight and recharged during the mission. Consider operation with one motor inoperative.

Requirements (analysis objectives for this input):
- Maintain commanded rotor power and synchronization.
- Maintain electrical supply, cooling, and lubrication.
- Assess failures across electrical, mechanical, and control interfaces.
