# Financial Impacts

**Preliminary analytical working draft; AI-assisted prose, not a student-authored submission.**

This analysis evaluates financial feasibility using cost per engineering-accepted FMEA as the intended outcome. The available pilot contains 24 generation attempts, including 14 successful drafts and 10 failed or uncertain attempts. Generation success is not engineering acceptance. Consequently, neither the manual before-cost nor the AI-assisted after-cost per accepted FMEA is currently estimable. Missing observations include manual creation and approval time, AI preparation and supervision time, technical review, correction, peer review, and acceptance against equivalent scope and quality criteria.

The initial engineering wage assumption is USD 64.89 per hour, based on national aerospace-engineer median pay for May 2025 (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics [BLS], 2026a). Private-industry compensation relative to wages supplies the benefits multiplier (BLS, 2026b). The selected loaded rate is USD 92.71 per active hour, using the versioned calculation or explicit override. The national calculation is illustrative, not a Sigma Airlines company rate, and excludes unmeasured firm-specific overhead. An already burdened company rate replaces the calculation rather than receiving another benefits multiplier.

Configured tariffs are gemini-3.8-flash standard_paid: USD 0.75/million input and USD 3.75/million output including thinking (Google, 2026). Recorded usage produces a known token-estimated or invoiced subtotal of USD 0.550623. Eleven attempts lack priceable usage. Actual billing remains unknown for all 24 attempts; invoice evidence is required to establish actual total expenditure. The subtotal cannot be presented as the complete campaign cost while billing is unresolved. Recorded attempt durations sum to 53.99 minutes; elapsed waiting is not automatically charged as active engineering labor.

Fraser's financial example illustrates translating an appropriate outcome into money and examining sampling variation (Fraser, n.d.). Here, the intended primary method instead preserves matched manual/AI cases and resamples independent system/reviewer clusters. No empirical 95% confidence interval for savings is presently estimable: one system's repeated drafts are not independent engineering systems, and no measured, quality-matched manual/AI cost pairs exist. Assumption-driven Monte Carlo intervals, when explicitly configured, must be labeled simulation intervals rather than empirical confidence intervals.

The next measurement phase should record active, passive, review, rework, and approval time separately and reconcile failed-request billing. Until those data exist, absolute savings, percentage reduction, annual savings, maintenance-adjusted net savings, break-even workload, and engineering lead-time reduction remain unresolved. Negative savings must remain possible. No financial benefit from avoided accidents, safety improvement, or generated-row counts is claimed.
